# Tramado manual

**English** · [Português](../pt/index.md) · [Español](../es/index.md)

Tramado keeps everything for each of your classes in one place: the lectures you record and their transcripts, the
documents and law texts, your notes, past exams and the class group's chat. It runs on your computer, needs no
account, and shares a class directly between classmates' computers.

This manual is for version 0.1 (release candidate 0.1.0-rc.1). It was tested on Linux; the Windows and macOS builds have
not been tested yet.

## Words used in Tramado

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Class | One subject you are taking: a folder with its lectures, documents, notes and exams. |
| Lecture | One recorded session of a class, with its recording, transcript, markers and notes. |
| Class group | The classmates you share a class with. |
| Organiser | A member who decides whether newcomers need approval, can remove people and can make others organisers. |
| Check words | Two words that identify a computer, for example *wavy-alpaca*. Compare them to be sure who joined. |
| Transcriber | The computer that transcribes a lecture for the whole class (only one per lecture). |
| Citation | A numbered chip in an AI answer that opens the page, article or lecture moment it comes from. |

## Keyboard

<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>K</kbd> (<kbd>⌘</kbd>+<kbd>K</kbd> on a Mac) searches everything and runs commands. <kbd>F1</kbd>
or <kbd>?</kbd> shows every keyboard shortcut of the screen you are on.

The pictures in this manual come from the example library (Welcome screen › **Try the example**): the classes,
people and documents in it are fictional.
